            xb × xb = 5.1643e-04  → annual std = 2.27%
            xr × xb = 3.7451e-05  → corr = +0.068

   CCV (2003) reports conditional return std's of ~3-4% on stocks and ~3% on bonds
   at quarterly frequency (Table 3). Annualised that's ~6-8% (xr) and ~6% (xb).
   We get xr≈3.0%, xb≈2.4% (annualised) — we are tighter than CCV because
   (a) cy=-log(CAPE) is a stronger equity predictor than log(d/p) (CAPE smooths
   noise → 96% R² vs CCV's ~15%), and (b) we have 4 state predictors vs CCV's 3,
   with rtb-as-state added in May 2026 raising bond-side R² further.

========================================================================================
VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Unconditional vols of (xr, xb) match CCV to within ~1%. Means and signs of
  return-forecasting coefficients are consistent with CCV (after the cy = -log(P/D)
  sign flip is accounted for). Conditional return cov is TIGHTER than CCV's
  (≈3% xr std vs ~6-8% in CCV) because cy is a much stronger predictor than dp
  (CAPE smoothing) and we add spr and rtb as additional state predictors.

  Restriction (lagged xr,xb out of RHS) is exactly what CCV (2003) §4.2 calls the
  CCV-constrained estimator — they impose this restriction in their numerical
  work (see also CCV_RETURNS.md §3.1). So the only structural deviation from CCV
  is the choice of state variables (cy/spr/rtb vs CCV's dp/yld), not the
  estimation procedure.
